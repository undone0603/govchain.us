VERIFICATION PIPELINE

How GovChain Works

Five steps from document submission to instant authentication — secured by cryptography, validated by AI, anchored to blockchain.

01

Document Submission

Agency uploads document metadata and hash to the GovChain protocol. The original document never leaves agency custody — only a cryptographic fingerprint is transmitted.

02

TruMark Sealing

A QRON TruMark — a cryptographic digital seal combining ES256 signatures, SHA-256 hashing, and visual QR encoding — is generated and applied to the document. The TruMark contains the document's complete chain-of-custody metadata.

03

Blockchain Anchoring

The document hash, TruMark signature, and custody metadata are recorded on the Polygon blockchain. This creates an immutable, timestamped proof of authenticity that cannot be altered, deleted, or backdated.

04

AI Consensus Verification

GovChain's 5-Agent AI system independently analyzes document authenticity. The Guardian, Archivist, Sentinel, Scout, and Arbiter agents each evaluate different aspects and must reach consensus before certification.

05

Instant Authentication

Any party — citizen, employer, court, or agency — can scan the TruMark QR code and receive a verified result in under 2 seconds. The verification includes full provenance: issuing agency, date, document type, chain-of-custody, and tampering status.

TruMark — Cryptographic Sealing for Government Documents

Every GovChain-sealed document carries a TruMark — a multi-layer cryptographic seal that combines visual authentication with blockchain verification.

Layer 1

Visual QR Encoding

QRON-generated artistic QR code that encodes document fingerprint, issuing agency, and timestamp. Scannable by any standard camera.

Layer 2

ES256 Digital Signature

Elliptic Curve Digital Signature Algorithm (P-256) provides 128-bit security. Each agency receives a unique signing key pair managed through HSM-backed key management.

Layer 3

SHA-256 Document Hash

The complete document is hashed using SHA-256. Any modification — even a single pixel change — produces a completely different hash, making tampering mathematically detectable.

Layer 4

Blockchain Anchor

The signature, hash, and metadata are recorded on Polygon. The on-chain record is immutable and publicly verifiable. Average gas cost: $0.002 per seal.
